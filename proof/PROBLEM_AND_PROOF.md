# The Ottaviani--Shapiro conjecture and a counterexample

## Original conjecture

For `k,l>=1`, let `tilde#(2k,l)` denote the maximum number of isolated real
zeros of a real nonnegative polynomial in `l` variables, of degree at most
`2k`, which is a finite sum of squares of real polynomials of degree at most
`k`. Ottaviani and Shapiro conjectured

```text
tilde#(2k,l) = k^l.
```

The number of square summands is unrestricted. A real zero counts when it is
isolated in the real zero set, even if it lies on a positive-dimensional
complex component.

## Exact quartic counterexample

Use the ten real variables `t,x_0,x_1,...,x_8`. Put

```text
q_0 = x_0^2 + t(t-8),
q_i = x_i^2 - (t-i+1)(t-i),    1<=i<=8,
P   = sum_(i=0)^8 q_i^2.
```

Each `q_i` has degree at most two, so `P` is a nonnegative polynomial of
degree at most four represented as a sum of nine squares.

Over the reals, `P=0` if and only if every `q_i=0`. Thus

```text
x_0^2 = t(8-t),
x_i^2 = (t-i+1)(t-i),          1<=i<=8.
```

The first equality forces `0<=t<=8`. For `i>=1`, the right side is negative
precisely on `(i-1,i)`. The eight excluded open intervals leave

```text
t in {0,1,...,8}.
```

Conversely every one of those nine integer values is feasible. At `t=0`, the
zero radicands correspond to `x_0,x_1`; at `1<=t=j<=7`, to `x_j,x_(j+1)`;
and at `t=8`, to `x_0,x_8`. The remaining seven radicands are strictly
positive, so each independently allows two signs. Each slice contains
`2^7=128` points. Therefore

```text
# Z_R(P) = 9*128 = 1152.
```

The real zero set is finite and hence every point is isolated. Since

```text
1152 > 1024 = 2^10,
```

we obtain `tilde#(4,10)>=1152>2^10`. This is a certified counterexample to
the universal conjecture.

## Real-radical certificate

For `j in {0,...,8}`, set

```text
a_0(j)=j(8-j),
a_i(j)=(j-i+1)(j-i),           1<=i<=8,
```

and let `K_j` be generated by `t-j`, by `x_i` when `a_i(j)=0`, and by
`x_i^2-a_i(j)` when `a_i(j)>0`. Each positive quadratic splits into two
distinct real linear factors. Hence `K_j` is the product intersection of
`2^7` real maximal ideals and is real radical. The sign argument above proves

```text
sqrt[R]((q_0,...,q_8)) = intersection_(j=0)^8 K_j.
```

This is an independent algebraic certificate of the real zero set.

## Complex-locus point

Over `C`, the quotient

```text
C[t,x_0,...,x_8]/(q_0,...,q_8)
```

is free over `C[t]` with basis

```text
x_0^e_0 ... x_8^e_8,          e_i in {0,1}.
```

Successive division by the monic private-variable quadratics proves both
spanning and independence, so the rank is `2^9=512`. Equivalently the nine
quadratics form a regular sequence and the ideal is an unmixed
one-dimensional complete intersection. There is no hidden zero-dimensional
complex component. Thus the isolated real points all lie on
positive-dimensional complex components. This is why a naive
zero-dimensional Bezout count does not apply.

## Stronger audited family

Let `k=2d` be even, `l>=3`, `m=l-1`, and `N=md`. Use variables
`t,x_1,...,x_m` and define

```text
A_1(t) = -(t-d)(t-N) * product_(r=1)^(d-1) (t-r)^2,
A_i(t) = (t-(i-1)d)(t-id)
         * product_(r=(i-1)d+1)^(id-1) (t-r)^2,    2<=i<=m.
```

Their simultaneous nonnegative locus is exactly `{1,...,N}`. At
`m(d-1)` internal integers exactly one `A_i` vanishes; at the `m` cyclic
block boundaries exactly two vanish.

Put

```text
F_d(x)=product_(r=1)^d (x-r)^2.
```

Choose `epsilon>0` small enough that, for every positive retained value
`A_i(j)`, the equation `F_d(x)=epsilon*A_i(j)` has exactly `2d=k` distinct
real roots, two near each root of `F_d`. When `A_i(j)=0`, it has exactly
`d=k/2` distinct roots. (One explicit choice is
`epsilon=eta_d/[2(1+M)]`, where `eta_d` is the minimum of `F_d` at distance
`1/3` from its roots and `M=max A_i(j)>=0` over retained indices.)

Set

```text
q_i(x_i,t)=F_d(x_i)-epsilon*A_i(t),
P_(k,l)=sum_(i=1)^m q_i(x_i,t)^2.
```

The one-active and two-active slices contribute respectively
`(k/2)k^(m-1)` and `(k/2)^2k^(m-2)` points. Hence

```text
# Z_R(P_(k,l))
 = m(d-1)(k/2)k^(m-1) + m(k/2)^2k^(m-2)
 = [m(k-1)/4]k^m.
```

Therefore, for even `k>=2` and `l>=3`,

```text
tilde#(2k,l) >= ((l-1)(k-1)/4) k^(l-1).
```

This exceeds `k^l` exactly when `(l-1)(k-1)>4k`.

## Exact boundary

This proof refutes the equality conjecture and proves lower bounds. It does
not determine `tilde#(4,10)` or the exact function `tilde#(2k,l)`.
